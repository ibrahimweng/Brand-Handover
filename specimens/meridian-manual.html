Meridian Brand Manual

This is the standard the system has to clear. It is a complete brand manual for a fictional tidal energy company, built from one SVG, five colour values, two typefaces and the rules a designer sets once. Every section is marked with who made it.

Read this as the floor, not the ceiling.
Meridian is invented for this specimen, so nothing here belongs to a real company. Everything marked as drawn by the system is genuinely derivable from a master file, which is the point of the exercise. Nothing on this page was placed by hand except the sections marked as yours.
Drawn by the system

Derived from the master SVG, the colour tokens and the rules file. You never touch it, and it redraws itself when the mark changes.

Set once by you

You decide the rule a single time. After that the system makes every instance of it, forever, without asking again.

Yours

Your judgement, your words, or an image you place. The system lays out the page and leaves the thinking to you.

01

The mark

1.1The primary mark

Drawn by the system

Meridian is a ring holding a tide line. The line is not flat, because water in a vessel is not flat. It is an arc struck at three times the radius of the ring, sitting a quarter of a radius below centre, which is what gives the mark its lift.

Meridian

Primary horizontal lockup · reversed on Deep

1.2Construction

Drawn by the system

The mark sits in a square of 12 units. The ring has a radius of 5 units and a stroke of 0.9. The tide line is an arc of radius 15, which is three times the ring radius, crossing the ring a quarter of a radius below its centre. Everything is a whole fraction of r, so the mark can be rebuilt from one number.

Every value here is measured off the master file, not typed in

1.3Clear space

Drawn by the system

Keep a margin of x on every side, where x is half the ring radius. Nothing enters that space, including type, image edges and the trim of the page.

04

The graphic language

4.1The swell

Set once by you

The pattern is the mark's own tide line, with the ring taken away and the curve repeated. Nothing new is invented, which is why it belongs to Meridian and could not belong to anybody else.

Rows sit at a fixed distance apart and every other row is shifted by half a repeat, so the field reads as moving water rather than as stripes. You made this decision once. After that the system holds the rule and cuts every tile, every density and every colourway from it.

The swell · Chalk on Deep · the default

4.2Density and scale

Drawn by the system

One rule, three densities. The repeat is tied to the size of the thing it sits on, so a business card and a site hoarding carry the same pattern at the same visual weight rather than the same measurement.

Fine · under A5

Report interiors, cards, anything held in the hand.

Medium · A5 to A1

Covers, screens, posters. The default if nobody chooses.

Coarse · above A1

Hoardings, vehicles, building wraps, exhibition walls.

The tile is seamless in both directions, so it can be cut to any shape without a visible join. Never scale a tile by hand to fit a space. Pick the density for the size and let it repeat.

4.4The icon grid

Drawn by the system

Icons are built on the mark's own proportions rather than a grid picked from somewhere else. Three numbers carry across and they are the reason the set looks like it belongs to Meridian.

The margin. The mark leaves one unit clear inside a twelve unit square. An icon leaves two units clear inside a twenty four unit square, which is the same fraction. The stroke. The ring is 0.9 in a 12 unit box, so an icon stroke is 1.8 in a 24 unit box. The curve. Every arc in the identity is struck at 1.25 times the width of its own box. In the mark that gives 150. In an icon it gives 30. It is the same curve at a different size.

Four keylines keep the set optically even, and they are a circle of 20, a square of 20, a portrait of 16 by 20 and a landscape of 20 by 16. A round icon drawn to the square keyline always looks too big, which is the mistake the keylines exist to stop.

05

Motion

5.1The curve and the durations

Set once by you

Meridian moves like water, which means it never starts or stops abruptly. The easing is a circular curve, taken from the same geometry as the tide line. The mark uses a shallow crop of a large circle. The motion uses the same circle over a longer sweep.

Tide out

cubic-bezier(0, 0.55, 0.45, 1)

Anything arriving or changing state. Leaves at speed and settles. This is the default and most motion should use it.

Tide through

cubic-bezier(0.85, 0, 0.15, 1)

Anything that travels a distance and stops, such as a panel, a page or the tide rising in the mark. Eased at both ends.

StepDurationUsed for
Instant0 msAnything a person is dragging or typing into. Never animate under a finger.
Quick160 msHover, focus, a checkbox, a colour change on a small element.
Base280 msMost things. If nobody chooses a duration, this is the one used.
Considered480 msA panel opening, a page turning, anything that moves across the screen.
Slow900 msThe mark building. Used once on a first load and never again in a session.

5.2The mark in motion

Drawn by the system

Once the curve and the durations exist, the build animates itself from the master file. The ring draws from the top, then the tide rises to its resting line while the ring is still finishing. It does not loop, it does not bounce, and it never plays twice in one session.

StepTimingWhat happens
Ring0–400Draws clockwise from the top on Tide out.
Tide300–900Rises from the base of the ring to its line on Tide through, overlapping the ring by 100 ms.
Rest900Holds. No loop, no pulse, no idle animation of any kind.

Exported as Lottie for interfaces, MP4 for video and GIF for email, at every size each platform asks for. A visitor who has asked their system to reduce motion is shown the mark at rest instead.

5.3What moves and what does not

Yours

A judgement about restraint, which no rule can make for you. Meridian sells predictability, so the brand should not fidget.

Moves. Something arriving on screen for the first time. A state a person just changed, so they can see that it worked. A number that has genuinely updated.

Does not move. The mark, after its one build. Body text, ever. Anything a person is reading. A chart that is being compared against another chart. Nothing on this brand loops, and nothing draws attention to itself while somebody is trying to work.

06

Photography

6.1The direction

Yours

No rule produces this. It is a brief, and it is the section that tells a client who you think they are.

Meridian photographs work, not weather. The sea is the subject of the industry and it is not the subject of the pictures. Show the concrete pour, the crew on the causeway, the turbine on the barge, the tide gauge being read. If a picture could sit in any renewables brochure, it is the wrong picture.

Daylight, and the weather it happens to be. Nothing shot at golden hour. Nothing shot in a storm. Meridian sells predictability, and dramatic skies argue against the product.

People are working, not posing. Nobody looks at the camera, nobody wears a hard hat they do not need, and nobody stands with folded arms in front of an asset.

6.2Treatment

Set once by you

The direction cannot be automated. The treatment can. You decide the grade once and the system applies it to every image that arrives, at every crop, without asking.

Meridian grades to a duotone between Deep and Chalk. Luminance maps onto the palette, so a photograph joins the brand rather than sitting next to it. The original is always kept, because a duotone is a decision and decisions get reversed.

6.3The mark over an image

Drawn by the system

This is the rule people actually break, and it is entirely measurable. The system reads the pixels underneath the mark, works out the real contrast, and raises a Deep scrim until it clears 4.5:1. On a bright sky that lands near 65 percent. On a dark hull it needs nothing.

No scrim · 1.1:1 · rejected

Deep at 65% · 4.6:1 · passes

The same measurement runs on any text placed over an image, using the body text floor of 4.5:1 from section 2.3. A layout that fails is not published. This is the one part of a photography section a machine can enforce, and it is the part that goes wrong most often.

07

Voice

7.1How Meridian sounds

Yours

Permanently yours. Every generated sentence in a brand manual reads like a generated sentence, and a reader can tell inside a line.

Say the number. Meridian sells predictability, so a vague claim undoes the product. Never claim the future. Say what has been built and measured. Plain words for a technical subject, because the reader is usually a planner or a councillor rather than an engineer. No weather metaphors, no tides of change, no sea of opportunity, ever.

Say

The lagoon produced 41 MW on average across 2030.

Not

The lagoon delivered outstanding performance last year.

Say

We have not built at this depth before.

Not

We are pioneering unprecedented depths.

Say

The tide is predictable four years out.

Not

Harness the raw power of the ocean.

7.2The mechanical rules

Set once by you

Underneath the voice sits a layer that is not a judgement at all. You settle each of these once and they never need thinking about again.

RuleSettingNote
The nameMeridianNever in capitals, never possessive. Write "the Meridian turbine", not "Meridian's turbine".
HeadingsSentenceSentence case everywhere, including buttons and table headers.
Units41 MWSymbol after a figure, word in running prose. A thin space between.
Dates3 April 2031Never numeric. A numeric date is read differently on each side of the Atlantic.
Figures0 to 10Words below ten, figures from ten, figures always with a unit.
Reading age14Checked per page. A planning objection is read by the public.

A lexicon sits beside these. Use rather than utilise, build rather than deliver, site rather than asset. Banned outright: outstanding, revolutionary, unprecedented, cutting edge, and every word in the weather list.

7.3What the system checks

Drawn by the system

Paste any copy and the mechanical rules run against it. Here is a paragraph a marketing team actually sent, with what the checker returns.

MERIDIAN'S Estuary Programme Delivered Outstanding Results In 2030. The lagoon utilised four turbines to generate 41 megawatts on average, and we will revolutionise coastal power by 03/04/31.
FlagRuleCertainty
MERIDIAN'SThe name is never set in capitals and never made possessiveExact
Title CaseHouse style is sentence case, including headingsExact
utilisedLexicon says useExact
41 megawattsAfter a figure the unit is the symbol MWExact
03/04/31Dates are never numericExact

OutstandingBanned word, and a claim with no figure behind itJudgement

Five of those seven are certain, because they are string matching. Two are marked as a judgement, because the checker can flag the word and cannot tell you whether what replaces it is any good.

The estuary programme produced 41 MW on average across 2030. The lagoon used four turbines. The next site is scheduled for 3 April 2031.

The rewrite was done by a person. The checker found all seven problems instantly and had no opinion whatever about the sentences that replaced them. That is the line, and it does not move.

What a full manual would still add

The pattern, the icons, the motion, the photography and the voice were all missing until you asked for them, and all five are now in the manual above. Here is what is left. Most of what is left can still be derived. The list that stays yours forever is short, and it is the list worth being good at.

Co-branding and endorsement

Partner lockups are arithmetic on the clear space rule you already set in 1.3, so the system can place any partner mark correctly without a designer.

The system
Data visualisation

The palette, the contrast floors and the type scale already decide this. The system can draw the chart rules and the accessible series order from what is in section 2.

The system
Templates

Letterhead, email signature, invoice, deck master. All of them are layout driven by tokens, so all of them come out of the same engine as this page.

The system
Illustration style

The same. A system can enforce a palette on an illustration and it cannot decide what the illustration should be.

Yours
Brand architecture

You decide how sub-brands relate. Once you have, the system builds every sub-brand lockup from the rule.

Set once by you

An earlier draft of this page said that photography and voice stay yours entirely. That was too coarse, and building them proved it. Each of those chapters splits three ways. The direction and the principles are yours forever. The treatment and the mechanical rules are decided once. The measuring and the checking belong to the machine, and they are the parts people actually get wrong.

So the honest line is not a list of chapters. What stays yours is the judgement inside every chapter, and there is some of it in all nine. The system draws, measures, enforces and exports. It has never once decided anything.
